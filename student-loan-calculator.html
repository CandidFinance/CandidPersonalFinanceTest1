Why write-off changes the maths

If your projected balance is heading to zero at write-off anyway — because your income is high enough that repayments are outpacing interest — then overpaying genuinely saves you money: it clears the loan sooner and cuts the interest you'd otherwise pay.

But if your balance is stable or growing (interest is outpacing your repayments), the loan is on track to be forgiven regardless of what you do. In that case, every extra pound you put toward it just reduces the amount that gets written off — it isn't "saved," because you were never going to pay it anyway. That pound is worth more left in savings, a pension, or an ISA.

When overpaying does make sense

Overpaying is worth considering when both of these are true: you're on track to clear the loan before write-off, and your loan's interest rate is higher than the return you'd get leaving that money elsewhere (cash savings, an ISA, or an employer pension match you haven't maxed). The calculator above checks the first condition and compares against your cash rate for the second — but a full financial picture usually finds a better home for spare cash than either option.
