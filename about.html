About Candid

Stop your money leaking away.

Candid's aim is simple: help people keep more of what they earn. It finds where tax traps, idle cash and unused allowances are quietly costing you, and works out what each option is worth, in pounds, from your own figures.

The why

Good income, good career, still losing thousands.

Plenty of people earn well, save regularly and still leave money on the table every year. Not through bad decisions, but through decisions nobody had the time to work out properly.

Pensions, ISAs, tax bands, allowances and mortgages all interact. Get one decision wrong and it can undo the benefit of another. Cash sits in an account paying less than it could. Pension tax relief goes unclaimed. A pay rise tips income into the £100,000 taper, where the effective tax rate jumps.

Between demanding careers and life outside work, few have the bandwidth to model each choice against every threshold, account rule and option across their wider setup. And traditional financial advice often means fees in the thousands or a six-figure minimum portfolio, which leaves millions to work it out alone.

Candid does that maths for you. It checks your whole setup against UK tax rules, allowances and live savings rates, and puts a pound figure on every option, so you can see what each is worth before you decide. The decisions are always yours.

The who

Built by Harvey Preston.

Harvey is a chartered accountant with ten years of personal investing experience. Before Candid, he worked as a strategy consultant at Strategy& (PwC's strategy consulting business), focusing on commercial due diligence, and as a venture capital investor at Channel 4 Ventures, focusing on consumer tech and fintech.

Why he started Candid

Most of the people Harvey knew were highly educated and well paid, yet had too little time, or too little knowledge, to manage their own money well. Over the first decade of their careers, that added up to a run of sub-optimal decisions that left them materially worse off. Candid is built to close that gap: the expertise to see what each decision is worth, without the hours it would take to work it out.
