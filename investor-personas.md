# Candid customer lives: assumptions and archetypes

Draft for review, 8 October 2026. Stage 1 of 3: agree these assumptions and the 20 archetypes; stage 2 runs them through Candid's own calculations; stage 3 turns the results into an investor page.

Everything here is hypothetical and illustrative. Fine for investors; not for consumer marketing without a compliance review.

---

## 1. What we're measuring

For each archetype, two lives run side by side, a year at a time, over 20 tax years (2026/27 to 2045/46):

- **Without Candid (inertia).** Things stay as they were at onboarding: cash stays in the same accounts on the same rates, pension contributions stay the same, allowances get used only as they already were, and life events are handled the way most people do (a fix rolls on to the lender's next deal two months late, a bonus is taken as cash).
- **With Candid.** Each year, whatever Candid would show that person (the same calculations the app runs), acted on at their **action rate**:

| Engagement | Action rate |
|---|---|
| High | 80% of suggestions acted on |
| Mid | 50% |
| Low | 25% |

**The benefit** is the gap in net worth between the two lives, after tax and in today's money. Reported three ways:

1. **While a member:** the gain in the years they pay.
2. **Lasting, by year 20:** what's taken while a member keeps working after they leave (a higher pension contribution, money in an ISA), but new decisions go back to inertia and moved cash drifts down to a mid-market rate.
3. **Value for money:** benefit for every £1 of subscription paid.

Each benefit is split two ways: **Candid today** (levers in the app now) and **with the roadmap** (levers not built yet), so investors can see what's real today.

**Two views of churn.** Each archetype leaves for a stated reason, in a year that suits their story (average membership here is about 5 years). Story-driven lifetimes flatter retention, so the summary will also show every archetype on a standard consumer-subscription survival curve: 40% leave in year 1, then 20% a year.

## 2. Economic assumptions

One base path, plus a low-rate and a high-rate path. Every archetype uses the same path. Starting points are today's figures: Bank Rate held at 3.75% on 16 September 2026 (next decision 5 November); CPI 3.1% in August; average 2-year fixed mortgage 5.98% (Moneyfacts, 5 October); best easy-access rates in our live feed around 4.5%, Premium Bonds 4.35%.

### Base path (by tax year)

| | 26/27 | 27/28 | 28/29 | 29/30 | 30/31 on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bank Rate | 3.75% | 4.00% | 3.50% | 3.25% | 3.00% |
| CPI inflation | 3.1% | 2.8% | 2.3% | 2.0% | 2.0% |
| Average pay growth | 4.0% | 3.8% | 3.3% | 3.0% | 3.0% |
| Average 2-year fixed mortgage | 5.9% | 5.6% | 4.9% | 4.6% | 4.4% |

Everything else moves with Bank Rate (BR):

| Rate | Rule | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Best easy-access savings | BR + 0.75pt | 4.50% |
| Best easy-access Cash ISA | BR + 0.90pt | 4.65% |
| Premium Bonds prize rate | BR + 0.60pt | 4.35% |
| Big-bank easy-access (the inertia rate) | BR − 2.25pt, never below 0.5% | 1.50% |
| Mid-market (moved cash after someone leaves) | BR − 0.50pt | 3.25% |
| Current account | 0% | 0% |
| Mortgage standard variable rate | BR + 3.75pt | 7.50% |
| Fixed mortgage spread | BR + 2.2pt today, narrowing to BR + 1.4pt by 30/31 | |

Other assumptions:

- **Investments:** 6% a year nominal after fees (about 4% after inflation).
- **House prices:** 2% in 26/27, 3% in 27/28, then CPI + 1.5pt (3.5% on the base path). London and the South East: the same for the first two years, then 2% a year. That covers Kwame, Nadia and Dev (London), and Ellie, Sophie, and Helen and Richard (South East).
- **Rents:** rise with average pay.
- **RPI:** CPI + 0.9pt until 2030, then equal to CPI (RPI reform).
- **Individual pay:** rises from each archetype's life events, on top of average pay growth.

### Low and high paths

| | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| Bank Rate | Falls to 2.0% by 29/30 and stays | Rises to 4.5% in 27/28, settles at 4.0% |
| CPI | 2.0% from 27/28 | 4.0% in 27/28, 2.5% long run |
| Effect on Candid | Rate gaps shrink, so cash benefit falls | Rate gaps widen, so cash benefit rises |

Rate gaps scale with Bank Rate, so the low path is the honest stress test for the cash story.

### Tax and allowances

Already legislated or announced (all in the model):

| Change | From |
|---|---|
| Dividend tax 10.75% basic, 35.75% higher (additional unchanged at 39.35%) | April 2026 |
| Savings income tax up 2pt: 22% basic, 42% higher, 47% additional | April 2027 |
| Property income tax up 2pt | April 2027 |
| Cash ISA limit £12,000 for under-65s (total ISA stays £20,000) | April 2027 |
| Unused pensions count towards inheritance tax | April 2027 |
| Plan 2 repayment threshold frozen at £29,385 | April 2027 to 2030 |
| Salary sacrifice: NI saving only on the first £2,000 a year | April 2029 |
| Income tax thresholds and personal allowance frozen | Until April 2031 |
| Inheritance tax nil-rate bands frozen | Until April 2031 |

Not announced: assumed to stay where they are in cash terms (ISA £20,000, LISA £4,000, PSA, CGT allowance £3,000, dividend allowance £500, pension annual allowance £60,000, Child Benefit charge £60k to £80k, childcare support cut-off £100k). State pension: 3.5% a year. Lifetime ISA continues as now (the government has consulted on replacing it; if it does, the replacement is assumed to match).

**Income tax thresholds after the freeze.** Fiscal drag is too useful to governments to give up, so thresholds aren't assumed to track inflation. Instead they're revisited in April 2031 and every five years after, each time rising by about half of the previous five years' inflation, then frozen again until the next review. On the base path:

| From April | Rise | Personal allowance | Higher-rate threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 (now) | Frozen | £12,570 | £50,270 |
| 2031 | 6% | £13,320 | £53,290 |
| 2036 | 5% | £13,990 | £55,950 |
| 2041 | 5% | £14,690 | £58,750 |

National Insurance thresholds move with them. The £100,000 taper point and the £125,140 additional-rate threshold stay frozen throughout, as they have been since 2010. On the low and high paths, the rises follow those paths' inflation.

All archetypes live in England or Wales, because the app doesn't yet handle Scottish income tax bands.

## 3. Revenue per member

£4.99 a month at launch, rising 15% every three years. Price rises apply to existing members too.

| Tax years | Monthly price | Per year | Per year after VAT |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26/27 to 28/29 | £4.99 | £59.88 | £49.90 |
| 29/30 to 31/32 | £5.74 | £68.88 | £57.40 |
| 32/33 to 34/35 | £6.60 | £79.20 | £66.00 |
| 35/36 to 37/38 | £7.59 | £91.08 | £75.90 |
| 38/39 to 40/41 | £8.73 | £104.76 | £87.30 |
| 41/42 to 43/44 | £10.04 | £120.48 | £100.40 |
| 44/45 to 45/46 | £11.54 | £138.48 | £115.40 |

The member's cost (counted against their benefit) is the full price. Candid's revenue is after VAT (20%, included in the price) and after payment fees. Billing: 90% through the App Store and Google Play at 15% (Apple's Small Business Program and Google's subscription rate; both charge on the price after VAT), 10% through the website at about 2%. Blended fee: about 13.7%.

| | Per month at £4.99 | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| Price paid by the member | £4.99 | £59.88 |
| After VAT | £4.16 | £49.90 |
| Candid's revenue, after store and payment fees | £3.59 | £43.07 |

So Candid keeps about 72% of the price. Referral commissions from providers are left out.

## 4. The archetypes

Year 1 (Y1) is 2026/27, when each archetype joins Candid.

### 1. Priya, 23: first job
Manchester · single, flat-share · graduate analyst, £32k · Plan 2 loan £52k · engagement: mid
- **Starts with:** £3k in a current account. Pays 4% into the pension; the employer matches up to 6%.
- **Without Candid:** cash sits in the current account; overpays the student loan £100 a month "to get rid of it"; stays at 4%.
- **Life:** Y2 £35k · Y3 new job, £44k · Y5 £52k, now a higher-rate taxpayer · Y6 buys a £220k flat.
- **Where Candid helps:** full employer match; a Lifetime ISA for the deposit (25% bonus); not overpaying a loan that's likely to be written off; a better rate on the deposit pot.
- **Leaves:** Y4, saving on autopilot and feeling sorted. Tests lasting value: the LISA and the pension change keep working.

### 2. Callum, 29: heading for the £100k trap
Bristol · lives with partner Ella (nurse, £38k) · software engineer, £68k and rising · Plan 2 loan £60k · engagement: high
- **Starts with:** £25k in a big-bank saver at 1.5%; £8k Stocks and Shares ISA; pension 5% by salary sacrifice, employer 5%.
- **Without Candid:** cash stays put; ISA topped up now and then; when pay passes £100k, takes the tax hit and loses childcare support.
- **Life:** Y2 £78k · Y3 £10k bonus · Y4 joins a start-up, £88k · Y5 buys a £450k house with Ella · Y7 £105k · Y8 first child, nursery from Y9 · Y10 second child · Y12 £125k.
- **Where Candid helps:** cash rates and the ISA; the bonus into the pension; pension sacrifice to bring income to £100k, which keeps the personal allowance and childcare support (30 funded hours plus Tax-Free Childcare, worth several thousand a year per child); whether overpaying the student loan pays at this income.
- **Leaves:** Y10, two small children and no time. The hero case.

### 3. Aisha, 34: NHS nurse, family of four
Birmingham · married to Yusuf (bus driver, £34k), children aged 4 and 7 · Band 6 nurse, £42k, NHS pension · Plan 1 loan £9k · engagement: mid
- **Starts with:** £6k in a current account; £180k mortgage, 2-year fix ending in Y2.
- **Without Candid:** savings stay in the current account; the fix ends and the lender's next deal is taken two months late, on the standard variable rate meanwhile.
- **Life:** Y2 remortgage · Y3 Band 7, £49k · Y4 Yusuf's hours cut · Y8 remortgage.
- **Where Candid helps:** an emergency fund earning a proper rate; leaving the Plan 1 loan alone; remortgage timing (roadmap); Junior ISAs (roadmap).
- **Leaves:** Y3, money tight, cutting subscriptions. A modest case that shows price sensitivity.

### 4. Dev, 41: self-employed electrician
Croydon · married, one child · sole trader, profit £55k and lumpy · engagement: low
- **Starts with:** an £18k tax pot in a business current account at 0%; no pension; £210k mortgage.
- **Without Candid:** no pension; the tax pot earns nothing.
- **Life:** Y2 profit £70k, now a higher-rate taxpayer · Y3 shoulder injury, five months off, profit £31k · Y4 £58k · Y6 £65k.
- **Where Candid helps:** the tax pot in easy access; a personal pension with 40% relief on profit above £50,270; an emergency fund before the injury year.
- **Leaves:** Y2; comes back in Y4 after the injury drained savings; leaves again in Y6.

### 5. Margaret, 62: cash-rich, rate-poor
Norwich · widowed · retired teacher, Teachers' Pension £24k a year · engagement: high
- **Starts with:** £180k across two big-bank savers at 1.5%; £30k Premium Bonds; home owned outright (£340k).

- **Life:** Y3 gives a granddaughter £20k towards a deposit · Y4 turns 65 (keeps the full £20k Cash ISA limit) · Y6 state pension at 67 · Y8 downsizes, freeing £120k · Y10 starts paying £12k a year for help at home.

### 6. Jordan and Sam, 31 and 30: saving for a first home
Leeds · renting · Jordan graphic designer £36k, Sam civil servant £39k (Civil Service pension) · Plan 2 loans · engagement: mid

## 5. App gaps found along the way (fixed 8 October 2026)

1. **Savings tax goes up 2pt in April 2027.** Now in `tax.js` by tax year; Assist and the Cash screen switch on 6 April 2027.
2. **The Cash ISA limit falls to £12,000 for under-65s in April 2027.** Also in `tax.js` by tax year; Assist fills a Cash ISA only to the limit.
3. **Dividend tax rates in `rentVsBuy.js` were out of date.** Now 10.75% and 35.75%.
4. **Pension projections assumed 6% growth in today's money.** Now 4% (6% less 2% inflation), the same as this model.
