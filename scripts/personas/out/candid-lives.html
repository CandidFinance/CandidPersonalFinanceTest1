How this is worked out

  • Without Candid, things stay as they were: savings stay in the same accounts, pension payments stay the same, and allowances are used only as they already were. A fixed-rate mortgage rolls onto the lender's next deal two months late.
  • With Candid, each year they're a member, the model asks the app what it would show them, using the app's own code, on that year's rates. They act on 80%, 50% or 25% of it, depending on how engaged they are. What they set up while a member, such as a pension payment or an ISA, keeps working after they leave; new decisions go back to how they were.
  • Better off by is the gap in net worth between the two lives, in today's money, after tax and after the subscription. Pensions count after the tax due on drawing them; student loans count as what's still to be repaid.
  • The economy follows one path for everyone. Base: Bank Rate 3.75% now, one more rise, settling at 3% from 2030. The low path falls to 2%; the high path settles at 4%. Tax changes already announced are in, such as the £12,000 Cash ISA limit and the higher savings tax from April 2027. Income tax thresholds stay frozen to 2031, then rise by about half of inflation every five years.
  • Revenue is £ a month, rising 15% every three years (£ by year 20). That's after VAT, and after app-store fees on 90% of members.
  • The standard churn curve replaces each story's membership with a typical consumer subscription's: 40% leave in the first year, then 20% a year.
  • Worth knowing: money moved into a pension is invested, while in the life without Candid the spare cash sits in the bank, so part of the pension gains is investment growth. Each path is one fixed set of figures, not a range of outcomes.
